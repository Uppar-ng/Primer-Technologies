Your safety comes first

Stay safe on Zaure

Simple habits that protect you — whether you're buying a phone, ordering in bulk, or booking a service.

The 5 rules to remember

  1. Inspect before you pay. See the item, test it, then pay.
  2. Meet in public. Shopping malls, busy markets, police stations. Never at home.
  3. Never pay in advance to strangers — no deposits, no "booking fees", no exceptions.
  4. Verified ≠ guaranteed. A badge means we checked some details, not that the seller is perfect.
  5. If it feels wrong, walk away. There's always another deal.

Meet in a safe, public place

Where to meet — and where never to meet

Choose a well-lit, busy public location. Good options:

  • Inside a shopping mall or bank premises
  • At a police station (many stations allow this — ask first)
  • Busy markets, fuel stations, or café car parks
  • During daylight hours if possible
Never: meet at your home, the seller's home, an unlit compound, or a secluded location — even if the seller seems friendly. If they insist on their house, walk away.

Bring someone with you if the transaction is above ₦100,000 or involves a high-value item like a phone, laptop, or vehicle.

Inspect the item before paying

How to check properly — especially electronics

Don't rush. A patient check saves you money and hassle.

  • Phones & laptops: power on, check IMEI/serial, test the camera, speakers, charging port, and buttons. Check if it's been factory reset.
  • Vehicles: check engine, chassis/VIN number, papers, and take it for a short drive. Consider bringing a mechanic.
  • Furniture: check joints, drawers, and for insects (especially wood items).
  • Fashion: try it on if possible, check stitching and material quality.
  • Anything with a receipt or warranty: ask for the original receipt and any paperwork.
Beware of "brand new sealed" items being sold far below market price. If a phone is ₦50,000 below everywhere else, ask why. It may be stolen, refurbished, or a fake.

Never pay in advance

No deposits. No booking fees. No exceptions.

This is the #1 rule. If you pay before seeing the item, you've handed over your money with no protection. Zaure is not an escrow — we cannot recover payments made directly to a seller.

Red flags that mean "do not pay":
  • "Send ₦5,000 to reserve the item"
  • "I'm not in town, but I can ship it if you pay first"
  • "Just send half now, pay the rest on delivery"
  • "I need a deposit to hold it for you"
  • "Cash only, but send transfer first"

Pay after inspecting the item, in person, at the same moment the seller hands it over. Bank transfer is fine — send it while you both stand there, and confirm it lands in their account before you leave.

Trust your instincts

If it feels too good to be true, it is

Pressure tactics are a warning sign. Legitimate sellers don't rush you.

  • "I have three other buyers waiting." Real sellers don't need to tell you this — they just wait for whoever pays first.
  • "Offer ends today only." Fabricated urgency.
  • "Don't tell anyone about this deal." Isolation tactic.
  • Seller refuses to meet in person. Could be shipping from abroad (unlikely at these prices), or could be a scammer with nothing to sell.

Walk away. There is always another listing. You never lose money by being patient.

Watch for buyer scams

Fake payment screenshots, overpayment, "agent" tricks

As a seller, you're just as exposed as buyers. Common tricks:

  • Fake transfer screenshots. Always confirm the money actually landed in your bank — not a screenshot, not a "receipt", not a "bank alert SMS". Log in to your bank app and check.
  • Overpayment scam. Buyer "accidentally" sends ₦500,000 instead of ₦50,000, then asks you to refund ₦450,000. Their transfer was fake — yours is real.
  • "My agent will come and collect." Then the agent arrives, pays in cash, and later the buyer claims the goods were never delivered and demands a refund from you.
  • "I'm a soldier/pastor/doctor." Fake credentials to build trust. Doesn't matter who they say they are — meet, inspect, pay.
  • Fake "Zaure verification" messages. We will never ask you to pay for verification. Ignore anyone claiming to be from Zaure who asks for money.
Protect yourself: Never hand over goods until payment is confirmed in your bank account. Take a photo of the buyer's ID for high-value items if they insist on credit.

Protect your personal information

What to share, what to keep private

You'll need to share contact details to sell — but only the right ones.

  • Share: WhatsApp number, general area (e.g. "Barnawa, Kaduna"), item photos.
  • Don't share: home address (use landmarks nearby), bank account details before a deal is agreed, BVN, NIN, or copies of your ID with strangers.
  • Use a public meeting place even when selling — never invite a stranger to your house for a sale.
  • Turn off "last seen" and read receipts if you don't want strangers tracking your activity.

Wholesale & B2B: extra due diligence

Bulk orders need more checks than single purchases

When you're spending ₦500,000+ on a bulk order, the checks should be proportionally bigger:

  • Visit the supplier's warehouse or physical location before committing. If they have no address, that's a red flag.
  • Check their business registration — ask for CAC documents. Real businesses have them.
  • Order a sample first. For agro commodities especially, one bag of 100 should match the whole order.
  • Use a written agreement — even a simple one signed by both sides with a company letterhead. Include delivery date, quality standard, and what happens if goods arrive damaged.
  • Never pay 100% upfront on first-time bulk orders. Standard is 30–50% deposit, balance on inspection before dispatch.
  • Bank transfer only to a business account matching the company name. Never to a personal account with a different name.
  • Get references. Ask for 2–3 customers who've bought from them before. Then actually call them.
Common B2B scam: "We can ship from Port Harcourt tomorrow if you pay full today." No inspection, no warehouse visit, no sample, no paperwork — just a promise and a bank account. This is how some people lose millions.

Recognise the 6 common scam patterns

The tricks you'll see most often in Nigeria

1. "I'm out of town — I'll ship it." Seller claims to be in another city/state and offers to send a courier after you pay. No inspection possible. Always a scam in 95% of cases.

2. The fake alert. "Check your phone, alert has entered." Buyer shows you a fake bank SMS screenshot. Never trust SMS — check your actual account balance in the app.

3. The overpayment refund. "I accidentally sent ₦X instead of ₦Y, please refund the difference." The original transfer was fake, the refund you send is real.

4. The military/pastor emergency. "I'm a soldier at the front line, I can only do bank transfer, my wife will collect." Fabricated credentials to bypass your caution. It's always a scam.

5. The "agent" pickup. Buyer's "agent" arrives, pays cash, collects goods — then days later, the "buyer" claims never to have received anything and reports you. Always get written confirmation from the buyer that their agent collected the item.

6. The fake Zaure verification fee. Someone messages you claiming to be from Zaure, saying your account will be suspended if you don't pay ₦5,000 for verification. Zaure never charges for verification and never asks for payment via DM. Report and block.

None of these tricks are new. The same 6 have been used for years. Now you know them — you'll spot them coming.

What "Verified" actually means

A Verified badge means we've checked certain details — usually phone number, email, and for business verification, CAC registration and physical address. It does not mean:

❌ That the seller will never scam you.
❌ That the item is exactly as described.
❌ That the seller is honest, reliable, or fast.
❌ That Zaure will refund you if things go wrong.

Verification is one signal, not a guarantee. Always do your own due diligence — inspect, meet, check. Trust, but verify.
